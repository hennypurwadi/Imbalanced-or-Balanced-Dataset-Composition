Assessing the Impact of Documenting Dataset Characteristics—Homogeneity Composition and Data Sparsity—on AI Model Transparency, Fairness, and Bias Evaluation
Examining Bias and Unfairness in Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) Due to Imbalanced Training Data and Misleading Effects of Small Sample Sizes

ABSTRACT

In responsible AI, ensuring transparency and fairness is very important for developing responsible AI systems. Data characteristics are becoming increasingly important within the field of Explainable AI (XAI), but currently it is not a standard practice. Documenting the characteristics of the dataset, including its homogeneity composition and Data Sparsity, is critical for a comprehensive approach to XAI. 
If the training dataset is imbalanced, machine learning models cannot make accurate predictions, while the small sample sizes can mislead the bias evaluation. Traditionally, responsible AI and explainable AI (XAI) have more concentrated on explaining model behaviors, including ethical, legal, and social aspects of the datasets. This study proposes the incorporation of dataset characteristics to be documented into Responsible AI and Explainable AI (XAI) practices, especially the homogeneity composition, whether the dataset is balanced or imbalanced, and data sparsity, whether small or large samples, for transparency at both the data and model levels, rather than only focusing on data sources and model explainability. The purpose is for AI to be open and accountable, enhancing fairness, and at the end is to increase people’s trust in AI systems.


RESEARCH QUESTIONS
1. How does the dataset homogeneity composition (balance vs. imbalanced) impact the AI model transparency and fairness in decision-making systems?
2. How does the data sparsity/ dataset’s size (Large versus small) influence the accuracy of bias evaluation in AI models, when the training data is class-imbalanced ?


There are four types of datasets to be compared and evaluated in this research: small, large, balanced, and imbalanced:
Small and balanced, Small and imbalanced, Large and balanced,Large and imbalanced

Literature Review:
The previous studies founded that the imbalance ratio (IR) influences the performance and stability of the model, especially in model like Logistic Regression, Decision Trees, and Support Vector Classifiers.

The previous study also warned that too small sample sizes can create significant bias. 

Terminology and Definition
These are performance metrics used in this study, which are Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1-score and Area Under the Curve/ AUC.

Methodology: 
- Synthetic dataset used for both quantitative analysis and comparative evaluations using Logistic Regression.
- Small Balanced dataset consisted of ten thousands dataset, versus Large Balanced dataset consisted of 1 million dataset, then investigate with LIME (local interpretable model-agnostic explanations).
- Small Imbalanced dataset consisted of ten thousands dataset, versus Large Imbalanced dataset consisted of 1 million dataset, then investigate with LIME (local interpretable model-agnostic explanations). 

Result Findings are align with previous studies' findings:
-Models trained on imbalanced datasets showed bias, favouring the majority class and ignore the minority classes. 
-There is Misleading Effects of Small Sample Sizes which provide a false sense of fairness, with models appearing to perform well on minority classes. The bias becomes obvious when the size of the dataset increase.

Discussion of Limitation
-This study were not perfect and has several limitations.
Recommendation for Future Research

Conclusion
For broader impact on XAI, this study recommends the documentation of dataset characteristics into Responsible AI (XAI) practices. By documenting dataset’s characteristic, will gain more trust from the stakeholders about the AI systems.

Recommendation for future research:
- To use Bias Mitigation techniques either over-sampling like SMOTE or under sampling.
- Exploring other models.
